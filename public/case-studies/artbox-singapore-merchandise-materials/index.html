Project snapshot

What the work needed to solve

For event merchandise, sourcing is not just finding a product link. Different product lines can involve different factories, sample rounds, colour references, labels, packing methods and shipping timelines. The event date is fixed, so every production and logistics decision needs to support the final handover.

Supplier coordination

Turn product references into clear requirements

We helped coordinate supplier communication around quantities, customization details, sample checks and approval needs.

Brand consistency

Keep colour, labels and finish aligned

Physical checks were used for colour and label details so the final items did not rely only on supplier photos.

Delivery readiness

Track packing and freight before the event date

Packing status, shipment milestones and final display readiness were tracked as part of the sourcing workflow.

How the project was handled

What we checked before the merchandise reached the event site

01 Sampling

Check the sample before bulk production

Custom items rarely move safely from product photo to bulk order without physical checks. Size and detail review helped reduce avoidable production risk.

02 Colour control

Compare print results with physical colour references

Screen colour and supplier photos can be misleading. Physical colour comparison made the approval discussion more concrete.

03 Label consistency

Keep brand details consistent across suppliers

Custom side labels were checked so different product lines could still feel like one coordinated event merchandise set.

04 Packing visibility

Check goods before they leave the supplier side

Packing photos gave visibility into quantity, carton condition and shipment readiness before the goods moved toward Singapore.

05 Freight tracking

Plan delivery around the fixed event date

Freight milestones were tracked so shipment progress could be judged against event setup timing instead of treated as a separate back-office task.

06 Event-site outcome

Turn sourcing work into ready-to-sell presentation

The final proof is not a quote or a supplier screenshot. It is the finished merchandise visible on site, ready for the event audience.
